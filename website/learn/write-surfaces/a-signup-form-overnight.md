# A signup form overnight

Illustration. This is not a customer story, and it is not a report of a real incident. It describes a generic public signup form, and it uses only the public product numbers already on the Attackfee homepage.

Imagine a registration form that stays open overnight. If creating an account costs the sender nothing, a script can keep posting signups while no one is watching the queue. By morning you have accounts to review, mail to send, and logs to sort. The rest of the site is still there to read. The cost is in what was created.

Now imagine the same form with Attackfee on the post. A person who is actually signing up earns a work pass in the background. That pass covers 20 actions for 15 minutes. Overnight volume that keeps going has to earn another pass, again and again, or pay when that path is on. Reading the site stays free. People never pay money.

This does not prove an account is legitimate, and it does not stop a funded attacker who is willing to keep paying the cost. It changes the price of leaving the form open. You still review what gets through.
